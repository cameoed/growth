# Growth tactics

Beings Growth helps you build a prospect list, decide which companies merit a closer look, and find a business contact route. The tactics below describe discovery and qualification supported by the code. Some require your own provider credentials and an approved scan. Outreach happens outside the app.

## 1. Build a prospect universe

- **Certificate Transparency discovery.** Enumerate historical hostnames under a source apex and qualify the live companies behind them.
- **Hosted customer discovery.** Resolve storefronts, tenant pages, and customer workspaces to operating brands.
- **Platform showcases.** Follow public customer galleries, storefront examples, and featured integrations.
- **Directory crawling.** Traverse category pages and profiles to find official company websites.
- **Agency portfolios.** Use case studies and client links to discover both agencies and the brands they serve.
- **Customer and partner lists.** Extract companies from named customers, testimonials, technology partners, and marketplaces.
- **Unlinked company references.** Retain name-only references for identity research without guessing domains.
- **Regional agency discovery.** Use OpenStreetMap to find nearby production companies, photographers, studios, and agencies.
- **Knowledge graph discovery.** Use Wikidata to find advertising and production businesses and verify their sites.
- **Search recipes.** Rotate sectors, regions, proof terms, date windows, and search operators for repeatable prospect research.
- **Cited AI source research.** Find and review potential sources with bounded web searches.
- **Mission-based Microlink search.** Look for companies in the selected category and adjacent markets, with optional size and domain filters.
- **Hiring and launch searches.** Find employers from recent creative hiring and new-company or product announcements.
- **RSS and Atom discovery.** Follow public feeds for newly mentioned companies and launch activity.
- **News discovery.** Use GDELT metadata and recent Hacker News launches to surface candidates with a verifiable domain.
- **SEC and federal streams.** Monitor Form D, S-1/F-1, 8-K, Regulation A, Regulation Crowdfunding, and other official public filing streams.
- **Advertiser-first discovery.** Start with Meta ad keywords, then verify which company owns each landing site.
- **Direct company import.** Bring a list of known URLs into the same inventory and qualify them with the same rules.
- **Evidence-based lookalikes.** Find similar saved companies using specialty, demand, vendor, and source overlap.
- **Source yield measurement.** Compare sources by the live, ranked, and contactable companies they produce.

## 2. Spot timing and recurring demand

- **Product release cadence.** Read dated release pages, changelogs, and app-store publications.
- **New product launches.** Look for launch activity across company pages, news, and public feeds.
- **Commerce activity.** Use public product catalogs and sitemaps to identify assortment and update patterns.
- **Subscription models.** Identify public subscription, retention, and recurring offer signals.
- **Campaign refresh.** Compare active creative groups and dated releases across observation windows.
- **Lifecycle marketing.** Analyze public lifecycle campaign archives for ongoing production demand.
- **Creative-format breadth.** Identify product photography, video, performance creative, and reusable asset patterns.
- **Creator programs.** Read public creator and ambassador pages for explicit content requirements.
- **Sponsored creator partnerships.** Use verified brand attribution for creator activity supported by company evidence.
- **Localization.** Identify language and market variants, translation activity, and international campaign footprint.
- **Owned media.** Track company-linked podcasts, editorial feeds, and public programming.
- **Corporate blogs.** Measure dated publishing activity and editorial structure.
- **Webinars and events.** Identify upcoming and recurring programs from dated first-party listings.
- **Expansion signals.** Look for new locations, market entries, and public partnership announcements.
- **Leadership changes.** Capture dated marketing appointments and promotions when supported by sources.
- **Financing and corporate changes.** Use public filings and announcements for timing and company context.
- **Explicit creative procurement.** Find attributable requests for outside design, content, or production help.

## 3. Qualify fit, scale, and distribution

- **First-party positioning.** Read company pages for what the business sells, buys, or delivers.
- **Client throughput.** Distinguish agency work delivered to clients from the agency's own advertising.
- **Buyer and referral fit.** Evaluate agencies as potential buyers or referral partners using different evidence.
- **Creative operations.** Check for an in-house studio, production staff, roster, contractor, overflow, or white-label language.
- **Public hiring.** Read company career pages and linked Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby boards.
- **Role mix.** Look for creative, marketing, growth, ecommerce, design, and leadership hiring.
- **Team scale.** Combine direct employee counts with separately labeled signals such as locations and open roles.
- **Workforce context.** Use optional provider data to compare marketing or design staffing with demonstrated demand.
- **Public labor disclosures.** Read relevant Department of Labor records as dated evidence with limited scope.
- **Operating stack.** Identify ecommerce, subscriptions, retention, attribution, experiments, reviews, and media delivery tools.
- **Vendor relationships.** Compare DNS signals with explicit company subprocessor disclosures.
- **Brand email posture.** Inspect public SPF, MX, DMARC, and BIMI evidence.
- **Public trust signals.** Read identity-bound security, trust-center, and compliance disclosures.
- **Design systems.** Find official public component libraries and documentation.
- **Brand governance.** Locate brand guidelines, approved assets, and media kits.
- **Growth measurement.** Read public event and instrumentation schemas from static material.
- **AI readiness.** Look for first-party AI product and workflow evidence.
- **Historical footprint.** Use bounded archive lookups to put current observations in context.

## 4. Research visibility and advertising

- **Technical SEO.** Read indexability, canonical URLs, metadata, structured data, content depth, and internal links.
- **Search visibility.** Use DataForSEO bulk estimates across unique company domains.
- **Detailed SEO checks.** Use a smaller capped follow-up for domain-level ranking detail.
- **Facebook and Instagram ad research.** Verify Meta advertiser identities and group repeated creative variants.
- **Google and YouTube ad research.** Use attributed visual ad evidence to qualify distribution and refresh cadence.
- **LinkedIn ad research.** Review attributable creative and advertiser activity.
- **TikTok commercial evidence.** Use coverage-labeled library results where available.
- **Buyer posts.** Check attributed professional posts for explicit creative hiring or procurement signals.
- **Landing-page verification.** Match advertising evidence to the official company and keep uncertain matches out of ranking.

## 5. Find a person or business contact route

- **Leadership and team pages.** Extract published people and roles with source URLs.
- **Business inboxes.** Prioritize company contact addresses according to their documented purpose.
- **Contact forms and phone routes.** Use first-party forms and structured business telephone links.
- **Rendered-page contact discovery.** Use Microlink to read eligible public pages that need client-side rendering.
- **Top-company contact batches.** Start with the next due companies, with a size and enterprise-footprint preference in the Microlink contact search.
- **Professional people lookup.** Use Treg to identify relevant leadership, marketing, brand, creative, content, and growth roles.
- **Work email lookup.** Run a separately approved lookup for qualifying business contacts.
- **Cited AI contact research.** Request a bounded lookup for a selected company's missing contact details.
- **Identity and purpose checks.** Match each contact to the company and distinguish business routes from press, privacy, and support.
- **Saved prospects.** Keep useful people, notes, and touchpoint history together for manual follow-up.

## 6. Prioritize and spend selectively

- **Custom prospecting missions.** Apply a brief to the shared company inventory with free local matching or optional AI setup.
- **One Sales priority score.** Order companies using evidence about fit, demand, timing, access, confidence, and risk.
- **Evidence freshness.** Weight durable facts and time-sensitive signals on different clocks.
- **Provenance and coverage.** Keep source URLs, observation dates, and incomplete coverage available for review.
- **Alias and creative deduplication.** Count companies and ad activity by verified identity rather than raw row volume.
- **Due-first scanning.** Fill bulk SEO, AI review, and contact limits with the next eligible companies when earlier results are fresh.
- **Provider budgets.** Set request ceilings and review cost estimates before paid research.
- **Result reuse.** Use fresh receipts and matching cached results before starting additional collection.
- **Resumable work.** Preserve completed work and scoped selections during interruptions.
- **Manual qualification.** Verify buyer facts and contact routes before acting on a score.

A score is a research priority. Advertising presence, staffing signals, and search estimates have different coverage and levels of confidence. Review the dated evidence before using a contact or drawing a conclusion.
